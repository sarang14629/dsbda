### Step 9: Evaluate the model

```
print("Mean Squared Error:", mean_squared_error(y_test, y_pred))print("R-squared:", r2_score(y_test, y_pred))
```

There are two important evaluation metrics.

1\. Mean Squared Error (MSE)

MSE calculates the average squared difference between actual and predicted values.

\\[ MSE = \frac{1}{n}\sum\_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat y_i)^2 \\]

- Lower MSE is generally better.
- Zero means perfect predictions.

2\. R-squared (R²)

R² tells us how much variation in the target variable is explained by the model.

\\[ R^2=1-\frac{SS\_{res}}{SS\_{tot}} \\]

- 1 = Perfect fit.
- 0 = No improvement over predicting the mean.
- Negative = Worse than predicting the mean.

Viva questions:

Q. What is model evaluation?

Ans. It measures how accurately a machine learning model predicts results.

Q. Which is better, higher or lower MSE?

Ans. Lower MSE is better.

Q. Which is better, higher or lower R²?

Ans. Generally, higher R² is better.

Q. Can R² be negative?

Ans. Yes, it can be negative if the model performs worse than a mean-based prediction.

# Correlation Matrix – Explanation (Data Analytics / Jupyter)

This image shows a Correlation Matrix Heatmap created using Python. It is used in Data Analytics and Machine Learning to understand the relationship between different columns (features) in a dataset.

The dataset shown is the Boston Housing Dataset, which is used to predict house prices.

Let's understand it in simple English, like a viva answer.

## 1. What is a correlation matrix?

A correlation matrix tells us how strongly two variables are related to each other.

For example:

- Does the price of a house increase when the number of rooms increases?
- Does the house price decrease when pollution increases?
- Are two independent variables strongly related?

Correlation values always range from -1 to +1.

## 2. Understanding the colours

Red / Orange – Positive correlation

One variable increases, the other generally increases.

Blue – Negative correlation

One variable increases, the other generally decreases.

Light colour – Weak or no correlation

There is little linear relationship between variables.

## 3. Understanding correlation values

| Correlation | Meaning                       |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| +1          | Perfect positive correlation  |
| +0.8        | Strong positive correlation   |
| +0.5        | Moderate positive correlation |
| 0           | No linear correlation         |
| -0.5        | Moderate negative correlation |
| -0.8        | Strong negative correlation   |
| -1          | Perfect negative correlation  |

Important: Correlation does not always mean causation.
